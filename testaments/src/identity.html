TESTAMENTS

Understand the Why.

A magazine that treats one person per edition as a work of art. Each chapter is hung like a private exhibition: twenty-four pages in black and ivory, set in a high-contrast Didone with a literary Garamond, framed by the quiet geometry of a classical portico.

This first phase sets the identity, three cover directions and the five recurring signature layouts, drawn to true print scale. All names, titles, quotations and photographs shown are placeholders. Nothing is written in a real subject’s voice.

The visual identity

The masthead, the palette and the type are fixed across every edition. What changes from chapter to chapter is the person: the photography, the pacing, and how long the silences are allowed to run.

The signature light

Hard sun through a colonnade.

This is the brand's one piece of atmosphere. In photography it is literal: each edition opens on the subject's own architecture in raking light. In layout it becomes faint diagonal bands of light across black Monument pages, at 4–7% white, never on ivory.

At most one architectural element per page, and never on a page that carries a portrait full bleed. The column appears as a line, never as rendered marble; the person is always the most solid thing on the page.

IV · Reflection

A line taken from the passage below, set as its title.

This passage is the subject’s longest uninterrupted answer about what they have learned, what they regret and what they still hope for. It is printed as one block, without the question that prompted it, so it reads as a letter to the reader.

It is set larger than the Archive text, at twelve points on a generous leading, and the measure is kept short so that each line can be read in a single breath. Where the subject paused, the space stays.

The passage is chosen with the subject, not for them. If they would rather a different answer stood here, a different answer stands here.

IV

The 24-page sequence

The chapter unfolds like an exhibition. The section page ranges follow the brief; because page 1 is a right-hand page, every section opens on a recto. Black pages, plates and ivory pages alternate so that dense reading always gives way to a quiet room.

AOpening

Name, chapter title, standfirst, credits.

BMonument

One quotation on black. Also used for section openers.

CGallery

One framed print on white, wall label beneath.

DArchive

Two-column interview, small archival print, rules.

EEssay

Three columns with a half-page plate; for narrative passages.

FReflection

Portrait and passage divided by the column line.

GPlate

Full-bleed photograph, caption in the corner, no folio.

HLegacy

Closing portrait with “Every Chapter Matters.”

JSignature

Defining quotation; the subject’s own chapter title.

KColophon

Credits, final reflection, edition statement.

Flexing for length: a short interview drops one Essay (E) page per section and gains a Plate (G); a long one converts a Gallery (C) page to Archive (D). The Monument, Reflection, Signature and Legacy pages never move.

Print production

PaperText: 170 gsm matte coated, high white, for deep photographic blacks. Cover: 350 gsm with soft-touch matte lamination; the monogram blind-debossed on the back.

Room V, continued
Plates

Photography and placement

Every photograph is treated as a work in the collection: numbered, captioned, and given the space a print would get on a wall.

  1. Light from one side. Window light or a single source; let half the face fall into shadow. No ring lights, no fill that flattens the room.
  2. The real place. Photograph the subject where their life happens. Keep the clutter that tells the truth; remove only what distracts.
  3. Between the poses. The frame after the pose often holds the moment. Shoot continuously through the interview, not only after it.
  4. Full bleed or full frame. A plate either fills the page edge to edge (G) or sits inside large white margins (C). Nothing in between.
  5. No type over a face. Captions sit in a corner of empty tone. The closing line on the Legacy page sits below the shoulders.
  6. Number every plate. Pl. 1 to Pl. n in reading order, with place and date. Archival prints add “collection of the subject”.
  7. Grain is welcome. Keep film grain and slight softness. Do not retouch skin beyond dust and sensor marks.
  8. One colour exception. Colour only by written approval, and only as a framed Gallery print.

VI

The digital edition

On a large screen the edition is read as the printed spreads. On a phone the text reflows into a single column, and the signature layouts become full-screen moments you scroll into.

Desktop ≥ 1100 pxSpread view, true page proportions, turned with arrow keys or a swipe. Body text is enlarged to a 17 px minimum by scaling the spread, never by reflowing it.
TabletSingle page in portrait, spread in landscape.
PhoneReflow. EB Garamond 17 / 26 px on ivory; speaker labels stay in Inter caps. Monument pages fill the screen in black. Plates run edge to edge with the caption beneath.
NavigationThe five sections are listed as rooms, with a progress line that runs down the left edge like a column.
AccessibilityEvery plate carries a written description from the photographer. Text contrast stays at AA or better; Stone is never used for reading text on ivory.

VII

Needed for Phase II

Phase II builds the full 24-page template and the master pages in production files. To set a real chapter rather than placeholders, these four things are needed.

01 · Decision

The standing cover direction: A, B (recommended) or C.

02 · The subject

Name, chapter number and the chapter title they chose, with their consent to publish.

03 · The transcript

The full interview as recorded, with speaker turns marked. It will be set as spoken.

04 · The plates

Selected photographs at full resolution, with place, date and photographer for each.
